01 / Study design

How the comparison works.

Three attribution models, each measured with a 14-day and a 30-day lookback. The model allocates order credit; the window determines which preceding interactions are eligible.

Last-touch

All credit goes to the final eligible interaction before the order.

Linear

Credit is shared equally across eligible interactions.

Algorithmic

Adobe’s statistical method allocates the credit. This study compares the exported results.

The data and comparison scope

I cleaned and joined four channel-level exports by marketing channel using Python, then calculated relative credit changes and order-volume ranks. The exports already contain the model outputs; this is a descriptive comparison of those allocations.

Every setting uses the Person container, which considers interactions at person scope rather than within one session. The lookback is measured backwards from each order. All 16 channels are retained, including digital advertising, messaging, search, and offline channels.

Data preparation, validation, and source files

Preparation

  1. Read the four CSV exports after their metadata headers.
  2. Remove exported totals and breakdown-label rows, and trim channel names.
  3. Join on marketing channel, keeping the attribution export as the 16-channel base.
  4. Calculate comparisons from full-precision values. Round only displayed results.

Checks

  • All 16 channel names are unique; the joined fields are complete.
  • Each of the three model totals agrees within its lookback window.
  • The five environment counts sum to each channel’s 30-day last-touch orders.
  • The website’s six-setting orders match the source attribution CSV.

Calculation definitions and glossary

Relative credit change (%)

100 × (alternative orders − last-touch orders) / last-touch orders

Both inputs use the same window. A +20% value means the alternative model gives 1.2 times the last-touch order credit.

Order-volume rank

Order channels from most to least attributed orders separately for each model and window. A positive rank change means moving closer to rank 1.

Conversion environment share (%)

100 × environment orders / sum of the channel’s five environment counts

This denominator equals the channel’s 30-day last-touch attributed orders.

Revenue per attributed order

reported total revenue / 30-day last-touch attributed orders

The export does not identify the currency or fully document the revenue metric’s configuration.

Correlation

Pearson uses the full-precision Lifecycle Index and algorithmic relative credit change. Spearman uses their average ranks. Both include all 16 channels and the 30-day attribution comparison.

Attributed orders
Order credit assigned to a channel by a specified attribution setting. Fractional credit can produce decimal values.
Percentage change
A relative difference divided by the last-touch baseline, expressed out of 100.
Percentage points (pp)
The arithmetic difference between two percentages. A change from 20% to 30% is 10 percentage points.
Container
The scope used to group interactions for attribution. This export uses Person.
Conversion environment
The order-completion setting: Mobile App, Website, Mobile Web, Point of Sale, or Call Center.
Correlation
A descriptive measure of association. Its value does not establish cause and effect.

Platform defaults vary: Google Analytics 4 defaults to data-driven attribution for event-scoped key-event reporting; CJA allows attribution settings at the metric and report level. This study uses last-touch as its comparison baseline.

The reported order totals also differ across windows.

The exports total 231,678 orders at 14 days and 236,183 at 30 days: an increase of 4,505, or 1.94% relative to the 14-day total. All three models agree on the total within each window.

The aggregate files do not explain this difference. The comparison therefore cannot establish that an identical set of orders was merely reallocated. The same-window percentages describe the reported results under each setting.

03 / Interpretation

What to take from the comparison.

01

State the attribution settings.

A reported performance comparison depends on its metric, model, container, and lookback window. These settings form part of the evidence behind the assessment.

02

Read credit change alongside volume and rank.

A percentage change describes sensitivity to the allocation rule. Baseline volume and rank show the channel’s position in the mix. Together, they provide a fuller view than a percentage alone.

03

Check what recurs across settings.

A recurring direction supports a more consistent interpretation within these exports. Its magnitude can still vary, and a threshold-based label can depend on the window or boundary chosen.

The scope of this interpretation

This descriptive comparison covers one month of aggregate demo data. It does not establish which allocation best represents a channel’s incremental effect on orders.

Journey-level records and reporting configuration would be needed to investigate the allocation differences. Cost, margin, and experimental evidence would be needed to assess economic or incremental performance. The supporting metrics below add context, but their incomplete definitions limit further interpretation.

Supporting analysis: conversion environments, revenue, and lifecycle metrics

These measures describe the channel mix alongside the attribution results. They do not determine which attribution allocation is more accurate.

Where attributed orders were completed

“Paid Search” describes a marketing channel. “Call Center” describes where an order was completed. In the last-touch, 30-day breakdown, Call Center has the largest share for 14 of 16 channels.

For Email, Website is the largest environment at 31.08%, although it is not a majority. For In-App Messaging, Mobile App accounts for 58.64%. These patterns add operational context to the attribution comparison.

Revenue context

Revenue per attributed order

Reported revenue divided by last-touch attributed orders provides another view of the channel mix. For example, the derived values are 909.44 for Paid Search and 826.63 for Email, in the export’s unspecified revenue units.

These figures are not ROAS or profit. Advertising costs, margins, and the revenue metric’s detailed attribution definition are not included in the exports.

Supporting analysis

Lifecycle context and credit change

Across 16 channels, the exported Lifecycle Index has a Pearson correlation of 0.547 and a Spearman correlation of 0.335 with the 30-day algorithmic credit change.

The positive association is descriptive and depends on the measure of association. It does not establish a channel’s role in a customer journey or explain why the model allocates credit differently.

Metric definition limit: the export labels Acquisition Rate, Closure Rate, and Lifecycle Index are retained. The underlying CJA definitions of the first two are not supplied. In the data, Lifecycle Index equals Acquisition Rate minus Closure Rate. This study does not treat those fields as verified customer conversion probabilities.

The takeaway

In these demo exports, last-touch produces a different view of channel performance. Assessing that view requires both the attribution settings and the consistency of results across them.
